\section*{1. SYC, square-root-SWAP, and TK2 columns}
We agree that a verified long word alone is insufficient for a reliable
reference when a much shorter implementation is known.

The previous SYC fallback used six pulses for CNOT. We derived and checked
an exact two-SYC controlled-phase chart with explicit local angles, all real
angle branches, and literal scalar closure. It supplies the native
controlled-block constructor and the CNOT fallback. The supplement gives
the algebra and time-ordered circuit. The identity is accepted by a
compositional polynomial certificate, not by a numerical fit. Cirq's prior
analytical native decompositions are now cited; the two-pulse count is not
claimed as new. DCX also has a checked literal one-TK2 word.

We have not completed exact short charts for the entire SYC and
square-root-SWAP columns. Instead, as the report recommends, the atlas now
annotates every remaining count gap against the independently recorded
numerical witness. The separate audit covers 75 nonlocal two-qubit entries
per column, finding verified witnesses for all 75 SYC entries and 24
square-root-SWAP entries. The other 51 searches are explicitly inconclusive.
All 99 witnesses were independently replayed using the catalog matrices
and the actual local alphabets; the largest literal entry error is about
$1.1\times10^{-11}$. No GP gate is used. For a symbolic family a witness
is one named parameter sample and is labelled accordingly, not a uniform
family bound or an exact symbolic identity. The operations, substitutions,
errors, failures, and versions are included in the package.

The unrestricted TK2 one-macro Cartan cap is also displayed on every
remaining two-qubit TK2 cell that exceeds it. Literal determinant
obstructions remain separate. Thus the one-DCX correction does not hide
other unfinished local-frame constructions. The CSV
\texttt{native\_column\_gaps.csv} records all audited cells and phase modes.
The displayed atlas words remain exact upper bounds; none of these
numerical fits or capacity annotations is relabelled as an exact word.

\section*{2. Motivation and extension beyond trace zero}
The introduction now motivates controlled observables through Hadamard tests,
controlled Pauli products, and reflection/oracle use in qubitization. The
claim concerns exact small-block interaction cost, without asserting a
measured algorithmic speedup.

A new proposition covers traces $\pm2$ and $\pm4$. Every trace-$\pm2$
involution is locally conjugate to $\pm R_\theta$, where
\[
 R_\theta=I-2|\psi_\theta\rangle\langle\psi_\theta|,\qquad
 |\psi_\theta\rangle=\cos\theta|00\rangle+\sin\theta|11\rangle,
 \quad0\le\theta\le\pi/4.
\]
Explicit words give eight CNOTs or six unrestricted two-qubit gates across
the family. Exact realignment gives
\[
 \det\mathcal R(R_\theta)=-\sin^2(2\theta)\,[2-\sin^2(2\theta)].
\]
Therefore the four-Ising theorem gives the interior CNOT lower bound four,
and the general two-gate exclusion gives unrestricted lower bound three.
Both upper words are checked with the Schmidt angle free.

At the product endpoint the controlled gate is locally CCZ, with minima
six CNOTs and five unrestricted gates. At the maximally entangled endpoint
it is locally Fredkin, with unrestricted minimum five and CNOT bounds
five to seven. The sign of the reflection adds only a local control Z.
Trace $\pm4$ gives zero interactions. This supplies normal forms and bounds
for every spectral stratum, while explicitly leaving the trace-$\pm2$
interior optimum open. It is not presented as a complete extension of the
balanced cost classification.

\section*{3. SQiSW positioning}
The invariant result is now a proposition. We cite Peterson--Crooks--Smith's
monodromy-polytope treatment (including its corrected version) and identify
Shende--Bullock--Markov's trace-invariant tests as the closest analogue.
Huang et al.\ retain credit for the known two-pulse chamber region.

The main paper now gives the square-root-free version. With
$a=\Re g_1$, $b=\Im g_1$, and $W=g_2+1$, membership is equivalent to
\[
 W\ge0,\quad W^2-8a\ge0,\quad W^4-16aW^2-64b^2\ge0.
\]
For unitary inputs, $1/\det U=\overline{\det U}$, so these are real
polynomial inequalities on matrix entries. The purpose is symbolic-family
reasoning. We explicitly acknowledge that numerical canonicalization is
already a small eigenvalue problem.
