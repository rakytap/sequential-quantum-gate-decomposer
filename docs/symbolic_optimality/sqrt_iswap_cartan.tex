\section{Conventions and the two-pulse middle chart}
Write
\[
 \A(x,y,z)=\exp(i(xXX+yYY+zZZ)),\qquad
 S=\Sqi=\A(\pi/8,\pi/8,0).
\]
First assume $\pi/4\geq x\geq y\geq |z|$ and $x\geq y+|z|$.
The middle chart of~\cite{sq} can be expressed without inverse cosines.
Put
\begin{align*}
 X&=\cos2x,&Y&=\cos2y,&Z&=\cos2z,\\
 d&=X-Y+Z,&r&=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2+Z^2-2XYZ-1},\\
 C&=(1+d-r)/2,&V&=(1-d-r)/2,&D&=(1+X)(1+Z)(1-Y)/2.
\end{align*}
On the regular chart define
\[
 c=\sqrt C,\quad t=\sqrt{1-C},\quad v=\sqrt V,\quad
 u=\operatorname{sgn}_+(z)\sqrt D/c,\quad w=\sqrt{XYZ}/c,
\]
where $\operatorname{sgn}_+(0)=1$. Then
\[
 c^2+t^2=1,\quad u^2+v^2+w^2=1,\quad
 U=S\big((uI+ivX+iwZ)\otimes(cI+itX)\big)S.
\]
The only $c=0$ point in this chamber is the CNOT corner
$(x,y,z)=(\pi/4,0,0)$. There the explicit branch is
$(c,t,u,v,w)=(0,1,0,0,1)$; no division by zero is evaluated.

The domain inequalities follow from $0\leq X\leq Y,Z\leq1$ and
$x\geq y+|z|$. In particular
\[
 r^2=(X-YZ)^2-(1-Y^2)(1-Z^2)\geq0,
\]
and
\begin{align*}
 (1-d)^2-r^2&=2(1-X)(1+Y)(1-Z)\geq0,\\
 (1+d)^2-r^2&=4(D+XYZ)\geq0.
\end{align*}
Thus every square root denotes its real nonnegative branch. The identities
\begin{align*}
 C(1-V)&=D+XYZ,\\
 4(1-C)VD&=(1-X^2)(1-Y^2)(1-Z^2)
\end{align*}
give the required normalization and the sign of the spin trace.

\section{Exact local-equivalence certificate}
For $W\in SU(4)$ let
\[
 \Gamma(W)=W(YY)W^T(YY).
\]
The implemented polynomial checks establish
\begin{align*}
 \tfrac14\operatorname{tr}\Gamma(U)&=c^2w^2+2ictuv
       =XYZ+i\sin2x\sin2y\sin2z,\\
 e_2(\Gamma(U))&=4c^4-8c^2u^2+4v^4-8v^2+2\\
       &=2(\cos4x+\cos4y+\cos4z).
\end{align*}
Here $e_2(M)=((\operatorname{tr}M)^2-\operatorname{tr}(M^2))/2$.
Both spin matrices are unitary with determinant one, so the third
characteristic coefficient is the conjugate of the trace and the fourth
is one. Their full characteristic polynomials therefore agree.
The standard two-qubit local-equivalence theorem~\cite{makhlin} supplies
local $SU(2)$ factors; the next section recovers those factors explicitly.
No numerical comparison is used as this certificate.

\section{Recovering the local factors through a real $3\times3$ tensor}
The spin matrix has the Pauli expansion
\[
 \Gamma(U)=k_0 I+\sum_{i,j=1}^3T_{ij}\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j.
\]
There are no one-body coefficients. For the target, the tensor is diagonal,
with
\[
 k_i=\cos2x_i\sin2x_j\sin2x_k+
          i\sin2x_i\cos2x_j\cos2x_k,
 \qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\]
Under local conjugation the tensor transforms by real rotations on its two
indices. Hence $T=W\operatorname{diag}(k_i)V^T$ for $W,V\in SO(3)$.
Write $a_i=\Re k_i$, $b_i=\Im k_i$. The product $a_ib_i=q$ is independent
of $i$. Consequently
\[
 H=(\Re T)^T\Re T-(\Im T)^T\Im T
     =V\operatorname{diag}(h_i)V^T,\qquad h_i=a_i^2-b_i^2.
\]
These are known symbolic eigenvalues; a polynomial root solver is unnecessary.
For each label $i$ construct its spectral projector as
\[
 P_i=\prod_{j\ne i,\ h_j\ne h_i}\frac{H-h_j I}{h_i-h_j}.
\]
Repeated nonmatching eigenvalues can repeat a projector factor harmlessly.
The implementation tests every eigenvalue-multiplicity pattern exactly.
A column $P_ie_p/\sqrt{(P_i)_{pp}}$, choosing the largest diagonal, gives
a unit vector. If labels zero and one share an eigenspace, subtract
$v_0v_0^T$ before choosing $v_1$. Set $v_2=v_0\times v_1$.

For nonzero $k_i$, the matching other column is
\[
 w_i=\frac{a_i\Re T+b_i\Im T}{a_i^2+b_i^2}\,v_i.
\]
If a coefficient vanishes, complete its frame with cross products and a
projected coordinate vector. All eight zero/nonzero patterns are explicit.
A collision $h_i=h_j$ implies $k_i^2=k_j^2$, since
$\Im(k_i^2)=2q$. Thus $k_i=\pm k_j$, and the degenerate eigenspace presents
no missing branch. The local-equivalence certificate fixes the orientation
in the full-rank case; cross products fix it in deficient-rank cases.

Lift each real rotation to a unit quaternion, taking the largest of
\[
 q_0^2=(1+\operatorname{tr}R)/4,\qquad
 q_j^2=(1+2R_{jj}-\operatorname{tr}R)/4.
\]
That pivot is at least $1/4$. Antisymmetric and symmetric matrix entries
recover the other quaternion components. The implementation verifies these
identities against the quaternion Pauli action as polynomial equalities.
The corresponding $SU(2)$ matrix is $Q=q_0I-i\sum_jq_j\sigma_j$.

Let $L=Q(W)\otimes Q(V)$ and set
\[
 R=\A^\dagger L^\dagger U.
\]
Then $\Gamma(R)=I$ and $\det R=1$. In the magic basis this is a proper real
orthogonal matrix, so the $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ double cover of $SO(4)$ makes
$R$ local. Its coefficients in the orthonormal quaternion basis
$(I,-iX,-iY,-iZ)^{\otimes2}$ form a real rank-one outer product.
A normalized nonzero column recovers the first quaternion and contraction
with that column recovers the second. Thus the final, literal identity is
\[
 \boxed{\A=L^\dagger S(C_0\otimes C_1)S R^\dagger.}
\]

\section{Three pulses and arbitrary real angles}
For arbitrary coordinates, fold each angle into $[-\pi/4,\pi/4)$ using
\[
 n_i=\left\lfloor2x_i/\pi+\tfrac12\right\rfloor,\qquad
 e^{i\pi n_i\sigma_i\sigma_i/2}
 =e^{-i\pi n_i/2}\bigl(R_i(-\pi n_i)\otimes R_i(-\pi n_i)\bigr).
\]
Simultaneous local Clifford permutations order their absolute values;
a Pauli frame on one wire makes the first two signs positive.
The scalar $e^{-i\pi\sum_i n_i/2}$ is retained explicitly.

For canonical coordinates choose a commuting one-pulse kernel
\[
 b=\begin{cases}
 (0,\pi/8,-\pi/8),&y>\pi/8,\ z<0,\\
 (0,\pi/8,\pi/8),&y>\pi/8,\ z\geq0,\\
 (-\pi/8,\pi/8,0),&y\leq\pi/8.
 \end{cases}
\]
Each is a fixed local conjugate of $S$. Canonicalizing the residual
$(x,y,z)-b$ puts it in the two-pulse chamber. To verify the inequality,
measure $x,y,|z|$ in units $\pi/8$ as $a,b,c$ with
$0\leq c\leq b\leq a\leq2$. The four residual differences between its
largest absolute coordinate and the sum of the other two are
\[
 a+(b-c),\quad (2-a)+(b-c),\quad (a-b)+c,\quad (a-b)+(2-c),
\]
on the branches $(b\leq1,a<1)$, $(b\leq1,a\geq1)$,
$(b>1,c\leq1)$, $(b>1,c\geq1)$, respectively. Each is nonnegative.
Multiplying the one-pulse kernel and the two-pulse residual yields three
pulses and four local layers, after fusing adjacent local factors.

Each unit quaternion has an explicit polar $U_3$ chart, including zero
diagonal and off-diagonal radii. Projective output records its scalar as
virtual metadata. Exact output absorbs that scalar into a two-$U_3$ local
word, replacing one existing local gate. The PhasedXZ conversion has its
own checked scalar. No physical GP operation appears in either basis.

\section{Reproduction and limitations}
The arithmetic graph is an exact representation with shared subexpressions;
its bindings introduce no independent parameters. Lazy branches avoid
evaluating unused singular denominators. The implementation files are
\texttt{symbolic\_sqrt\_iswap\_cartan.py} and
\texttt{symbolic\_expression\_program.py}. Generate and replay the archive with
\begin{verbatim}
python examples/decomposition/symbolic_sqrt_iswap_frontier.py \
  --output benchmarks/symbolic/sqrt_iswap_frontier_20261107.json.gz
python examples/decomposition/symbolic_sqrt_iswap_frontier.py \
  --verify benchmarks/symbolic/sqrt_iswap_frontier_20261107.json.gz
\end{verbatim}
The archive records source hashes, the code commit, twenty regenerated
certificates, and matching uniform-budget witnesses. The independent
regressions include full matrices at poles, repeated eigenvalues, fold
boundaries, negative and out-of-chamber angles, both native bases, both
phase policies, and all five free families. Regression samples supplement
the component identities and local-equivalence theorem; they do not
certify a symbolic family.

The remaining work is substantial local-score optimization and broader
global-optimality proofs. Neither the three-pulse construction nor the
earlier all-phase Clifford proof establishes global optimality throughout
the whole $327\times32$ grid. The existing scored archive and atlas retain
their previous version until shared-expression scores are evaluated under
the existing objective without alias substitution.
